HCI Assignment 1 (Part II): Empirical Analysis Report

1. Scenario & Real-World HCI Problem

In modern vehicles, physical rotary knobs are increasingly replaced by central infotainment touchscreens. In real-world driving contexts, drivers must prioritize road awareness and can only afford brief glances to interact with cabin controls (e.g., air conditioning). If touch targets are too small or positioned far from the driver's reach, target acquisition time increases significantly, leading to prolonged eyes-off-road time and increased safety risks.

3. Empirical Model & Derived Formula

Averaging repeated trials across all 27 conditions and performing linear regression under the Shannon formulation yields:

Model: MT = 306.55 + 136.63 × log2(A/W + 1)
Goodness of Fit: R² = 0.5019
Baseline Intercept (a): 306.55 ms
Processing Slope (b): 136.63 ms/bit

Figure: Linear regression scatter plot mapping Index of Difficulty (ID, bits) to Movement Time (MT, ms).

4. Discussion & Findings

The baseline latency (a = 306.55 ms) aligns closely with human physiological reaction time (~200 ms for visual stimuli) combined with motor initiation. The positive slope (b = 136.63 ms/bit) verifies Fitts' Law. The moderate R² (0.5019) is primarily driven by high-difficulty trials (ID ≈ 4.5 bits), where targets with small widths (30 px) at long distances prompted frequent overshooting and corrective sub-movements, causing movement times to exceed 1200 ms.
